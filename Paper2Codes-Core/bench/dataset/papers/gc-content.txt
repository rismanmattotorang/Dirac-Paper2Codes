GC Content and Reverse Complement of DNA Sequences

Abstract. We describe two fundamental operations on DNA sequences: computing the
GC content and forming the reverse complement.

1. GC content. Given a DNA sequence S over the alphabet {A, C, G, T}, the GC
content is the fraction of bases that are G or C:

  Equation (1): GC(S) = (count(G) + count(C)) / length(S)

2. Reverse complement. The complement maps A<->T and C<->G. The reverse
complement reverses the sequence and complements each base.

3. Implementation notes. The implementation should compute GC content as a value
in [0, 1] and produce the reverse complement, handling upper/lower case and
rejecting characters outside the nucleotide alphabet. Validate that the reverse
complement of the reverse complement is the original sequence.
